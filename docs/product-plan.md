# Product plan — Recall for Interviews (working title)

## Product thesis

Help engineers recall a concept without cues and explain it clearly under interview conditions. Start with Brazilian software engineers applying abroad; choose interview language independently of job location and interface language. Default international practice to English with optional Portuguese explanations. Local roles and other tech disciplines stay on the roadmap.

## Evidence and limits

Roediger and Karpicke observed better delayed recall after retrieval practice than restudy in experiments with prose passages.[1] Cepeda et al. observed that the best review gap depended on the time until the retention test.[2] Therefore ask for an answer before showing feedback and space later attempts; the initial 1/7/30-day policy is a product hypothesis, not an optimal schedule established by science. These studies do not prove better interview outcomes. Amazon's public prep guide covers coding and system design; use it for topic selection, not as evidence of question frequency or permission to copy questions.[3]

## Phases and acceptance

| Phase | Experience | Exit gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — iOS core | Onboarding → Today → question → explanation → next review. Original Software Engineering deck: system design, networking, databases and algorithms. Written answers get a reference answer, checklist and self-rating; multiple choice gets exact grading and explanations. | A physical iPhone completes the flow offline, including relaunch with saved progress. Tests cover repeated misses, progression, calendar/DST, duplicate submissions and no due cards. |
| 2 — Personalization | Topic and question-type filters, daily target, custom recurrence, optional local reminders, and short mixed-question mock sessions with a recap. | Filtering/reminders do not silently reschedule cards. Invalid intervals are rejected, settings preview future due dates, and mock sessions do not change the review queue. |
| 3 — Spoken practice | Optional English or Portuguese recording, replay, delete, transcript where supported. Separate technical correctness (rubric) from communication (clarity, structure and word choice). | Denied microphone, failed transcription and low confidence preserve text/self-review. Recordings stay on-device by default; any future external processing needs opt-in, retention and deletion policy. Compare feedback with human ratings before enabling automated grading; do not penalize accent. |
| 4 — Release | TestFlight pilot; Android compatibility and release follow iOS validation. Payments and App Store submission are separate decisions after product validation. | Measure whether users return for due reviews and can answer an unseen follow-up, not only card accuracy. Capture an English demo of the real app for the portfolio. No App Store submission or visibility change in this initial scope. |

## Scheduler contract (proposed)

New card + correct → 7 calendar days; second and later consecutive correct → 30 days. Any miss → tomorrow, reset streak; partial counts as miss. Due dates are saved local civil dates using the device's timezone at the attempt; a timezone change does not reinterpret stored dates. Queue overdue cards oldest-first before new cards. Save review history and next-due state in one SQLite transaction with a unique attempt ID. Configured intervals are bounded positive days, strictly ascending, applied to future attempts only; never silently rewrite existing due dates. No scheduler update before a rating, on back navigation, or during mock practice.

## UX direction

Prefer a dedicated, one-question study screen over a dashboard-first quiz grid: fewer context switches and more room for reasoning; topic browsing remains a secondary tab. Today shows due count, track, and one primary action: Start review. Study keeps progress above the prompt and one primary action below the answer. Feedback preserves the user's answer alongside a short explanation and explicit next review date. No timers or auto-advance in daily review. Design for Dynamic Type, VoiceOver reading/focus order, non-color-only status, light/dark contrast and reduced motion. Validate low-fidelity screens for onboarding, review, feedback and empty queue before implementing UI.

## Technical direction and boundaries

Recommend Expo / React Native and TypeScript with SQLite, offline-first and no account required for the first version. Keep a pure scheduler independent of UI/storage, versioned original deck content, and per-attempt history. Before application code: review architecture and product scope independently, then validate an Expo dev build on iPhone. Initial implementation scope: `app/`, `src/{study,scheduler,storage,content}/`, `tests/` and Expo project configuration. No backend, paid model, account sync, question scraping, or billing in the first increment. Public repo until actual App Store launch; making it private later does not recall public copies.

## Language decision (supersedes all older language requirements)

English is the default. The user can switch between English and Brazilian Portuguese; persist the choice locally. Each mode localizes the whole experience: onboarding, settings, dates, accessibility labels, prompts, options, reference answers, checklists, explanations and distractor feedback. No mixed-language content fallback. Changing language preserves progress because localized variants share card IDs, and does not convert a draft written by the user. Technical terms and code identifiers may remain literal where appropriate. Verify all study screens in both modes and verify the selection survives relaunch. Validate both complete locale variants before a private deck is eligible for release.

## Phase 1 implementation contract (takes precedence over summaries above except the language decision)

- Scheduling starts from the local civil date of the attempt. Wrong or partial resets the streak and schedules +1 day; correct from streak zero schedules +7 days; later consecutive correct schedules +30 days. Date strings are immutable YYYY-MM-DD; midnight in the current device timezone starts a new day. New cards are limited to five first completed attempts per local day. Today shows due (date ≤ today) and available new counts separately; order due date then stable ID, followed by new cards in deck order. Snapshot the queue on Start; no extra cards on completion. Show session summary and next scheduled date; distinguish an empty deck from all caught up. Refresh eligibility on foreground/next session. No undo in Phase 1; unfinished prompts and drafts are discarded on relaunch, with disclosure before exit; rated attempts persist.
- Generate an attempt ID when presenting a prompt. In an exclusive Expo SQLite transaction, return an existing result for that ID without modifying state; otherwise compare the expected card revision and reject stale attempts with a queue refresh. Store stable card ID, deck revision, rating, UTC instant, timezone, civil date, applied interval and resulting state. Use forward-only `PRAGMA user_version` migrations; do not assume connection-specific PRAGMAs propagate across connections. Keep the scheduler platform-independent. Run `npm test` against the pure scheduler and same SQL using Node's SQLite, including New York DST, migration, duplicate/stale commits and an empty/due/new queue. Exercise the native transaction separately. Pin the SDK in lockfile; simulator dev spike first, then signed Release on physical iPhone in airplane mode: rate a card, force quit, relaunch, verify due date and count without Metro.
- UI and explanations in pt-BR; questions and reference answers in English in Phase 1. Local interview language and EN UI are later options. Onboarding shows purpose, covered topics and review rules; no job-location question without an actionable choice. For open prompts, answer mentally, aloud without recording, or in optional text. Reveal is explicit; “I don't know” opens feedback. Self-rating: Correct only if all required checklist items were met; Partial if some were met; Missed otherwise. Multiple-choice cards use exact grading, reveal why distractors fail, and are distinct practice items (never advance an open-response card). Topic browsing moves to Phase 2 and does not affect scheduling.
- Versioned deck schema: stable ID, topic, type, bilingual-capable prompt/reference/explanation fields, checklist items marked required or optional, optional choices and correct choice, provenance and review status. No missing required locale fields in the shipped deck. Keep progress on typo edits; use a new ID for a changed learning objective, archive removed IDs without deleting history. Ship at least six original reviewed items per launch topic with both formats; a separate unpublished follow-up per topic tests transfer during the pilot. Public repo contains sample content only; no open-source license grant for code or sample content at this stage. Preserve behavioral interviews as later scope.
- Before styling production screens, validate onboarding, first review, feedback and caught-up low-fidelity flows with three target users; all should start, reveal, rate and locate the next review unaided after any redesign. Release gate: same path with VoiceOver, largest supported accessibility text without clipped actions, status communicated without color, and Reduce Motion honored. Capture real iPhone evidence; wireframes are labeled as such.
- Before pilot, compare the 30-day interval against interview dates; pilot hypothesis: over two weeks, at least half of ten recruited users return for a due review and answer at least one unseen follow-up adequately. Use consented local summary exports without raw answers/audio; interview participants about willingness to pay. TestFlight requires eligible Apple Developer membership; begin with internal testers when roles permit, use external review only when authorized. Neither store publication nor billing is authorized here. Audio starts with separate self-rubrics; only technical rating schedules. If required on-device recognition is unsupported, no transcript and no upload. Android smoke test follows iOS; Play Store publication is out of scope.

### Storage and content gates

- Serialize commits in the app and disable rating while pending. The SQL itself enforces unique attempt IDs and compare-and-swap on the progress revision (distinct from the deck revision); zero affected rows means stale and refreshes the queue. Configure connection-specific PRAGMAs on the handle actually used for writes, before BEGIN. Do not assume Expo's exclusive helper uses `BEGIN EXCLUSIVE`. On lock contention, retry the whole operation at most three times after rereading the attempt ID; after failure, keep feedback visible and retry with the same ID. Native tests submit the same ID concurrently and verify one row and identical results, then submit a stale revision and verify no change and a refreshed queue.
- Pin Node 22.13+; add local calendar tests for timezone changes without rewriting stored dates. Today shows next due date after relaunch when caught up. Disclose draft behavior before entry. Use explicit English accessibility language for English text, AX5, and check light/dark contrast.
- Public builds use versioned fixture cards. Private Release builds require an explicit deck directory outside this checkout and fail closed if it is absent or invalid; the identical validator runs in tests on fixtures and in Release against the selected deck. No private deck input is copied into tracked paths. Per topic require at least four open and two multiple-choice cards; prompts, reference, options and required checklist in English; explanations and distractor reasons in pt-BR. A changed correct option or required checklist meaning requires a new ID. An independent engineer checks accuracy, trade-offs and rubric completeness before a card ships. Follow-ups live outside app assets and are assessed in moderated sessions against their required checklist. Export only aggregate sessions/due reviews, not exact dates, timezone, raw answers or audio.
